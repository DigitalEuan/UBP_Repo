Abstract
We investigate whether the extended binary Golay code, the Leech lattice, and Barnes-Wall lattices can naturally encode musical consonance. While the Circle-of-Fifths Gray encoding through Golay produces r = 0.8674 for interval consonance, the code's three-distance constraint makes chord differentiation provably impossible. Dimensional expansion through Leech and Barnes-Wall lattices preserves but never enriches this signal. The consonance signal is found to reside in prime number theory: pure JI exponent vectors achieve r = 0.9613 for intervals, and a Jaccard-based Prime-Layer Harmonic Module on Mersenne/Fermat mod-144 residue proximity sets achieves r = +0.8244 for chords—the first positive chord differentiation in the study. The mechanism is traced to the decomposition 12-TET = 7 + 5 = (23−1) + F0, showing that the octave naturally partitions into a Mersenne number and a Fermat prime.
