### 2. Numerically Stable Archimedes Method (`pi_archimedes_correct_stable`)

The core of this project is the `archimedes_via_half_angle` function (`i52iLX00WCCC`), which implements Archimedes' method to approximate π. The crucial innovation here is the use of a "half-angle tracking" strategy to overcome the severe numerical instability often encountered in traditional Archimedes implementations, especially after many iterations.

**Traditional Problem:** The standard method of calculating the side lengths of inscribed/circumscribed polygons often involves subtracting nearly equal numbers (e.g., `1 - cos(small_angle)`), leading to catastrophic cancellation and rapid loss of precision in floating-point arithmetic.

**Solution (Half-Angle Tracking):** Instead of directly tracking polygon side lengths, this implementation tracks `sin(θ/2)`, where `θ` is the angle subtended by one side of the polygon at the center of the circle. The doubling formula used is:

`sin(θ_new/2) = sin(θ_old/2) / sqrt(2 * (1 + cos(θ_old/2)))`

where `cos(θ_old/2)` is derived from `sqrt(1 - sin²(θ_old/2))`. This approach keeps all intermediate values within a stable range ([0, 1]), preventing catastrophic cancellation and maintaining precision across many iterations.

**Validation:** The module includes `test_numerical_stability()` and `convergence_rate_analysis()` functions which demonstrate:

*   **Stability:** The method maintains high accuracy (within machine precision) even after 40+ doublings, unlike unstable methods which fail much earlier.
*   **Quadratic Convergence:** The error reduction ratio between successive iterations consistently hovers around 0.25 (i.e., the error is quartered with each doubling of polygon sides), which is characteristic of quadratic convergence.
