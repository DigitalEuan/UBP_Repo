\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a Phase 3 investigation into the universality of geometric patterns discovered in higher-order rainbows, proposing that these patterns are not unique to optics but are fundamental signatures of the Universal Binary Principle (UBP) information geometry. We frame the rainbow as a **transducer phenomenon**, where light and water act as optimal transducers for the underlying geometric structure of reality. We develop a theoretical framework interpreting the 200-order rainbow spiral as a **geometric low-density parity-check (LDPC) code** in angular phase space, stabilized by the Klein four-group (V₄). We then test for these UBP signatures (42°, 6φ, 10×6φ, 4-fold symmetry, 200/256 quantization) across five independent physical systems: protein folding, the quantum Hall effect, CMB anisotropy, neural oscillations, and quasicrystals. Our analysis reveals a **significant (>2σ) peak at -42° in the ψ torsion angle distribution of protein α-helices**, suggesting the dodecahedral constraint is active in protein folding. Furthermore, we find suggestive evidence in the other four systems, including the 7/9 quantum Hall state (within 0.5% of the 200/256 ratio) and the 3σ alignment of the CMB quadrupole/octupole. These findings support the hypothesis that the same geometric error-correcting code governs the structure of reality across vastly different scales and physical domains.
\end{abstract}
