GPS Time Dilation - Real-World Data
===================================

Source: Ohio State University Astronomy Department
URL: https://www.astronomy.ohio-state.edu/pogge.1/Ast162/Unit5/gps.html

GPS Satellite Parameters:
- Orbital altitude: ~20,000 km above Earth surface
- Orbital speed: ~14,000 km/hour
- Orbital period: ~12 hours

Time Dilation Effects (per day):

1. Special Relativity (velocity effect):
   - Satellites moving relative to ground observers
   - Effect: Clocks run SLOWER by ~7 microseconds/day
   - Cause: Time dilation due to relative motion

2. General Relativity (gravitational effect):
   - Satellites in weaker gravitational field than Earth surface
   - Effect: Clocks run FASTER by ~45 microseconds/day
   - Cause: Gravitational time dilation

3. Combined Net Effect:
   - Net effect: +38 microseconds/day (45 - 7 = 38)
   - Clocks on satellites tick FASTER than ground clocks
   - This equals 38,000 nanoseconds per day
   - Required accuracy: 20-30 nanoseconds for GPS precision

Critical Points:
- Without relativistic corrections, GPS would accumulate ~10 km error per day
- Both special and general relativity effects are essential
- GPS system pre-corrects satellite clocks to account for these effects
- This is direct experimental validation of Einstein's theories

Numerical Values for UBP Validation:
=====================================

Time dilation per day: 38 × 10^-6 seconds = 3.8 × 10^-5 seconds
Time dilation rate: 38 μs / 86400 s = 4.398 × 10^-10 (fractional change)

For 1 second of satellite time:
- Accumulated dilation: 4.398 × 10^-10 seconds
- This is the "real-world" time dilation we can test against UBP predictions
