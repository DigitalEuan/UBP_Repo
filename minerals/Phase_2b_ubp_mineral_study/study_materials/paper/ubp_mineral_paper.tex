\begin{abstract}
Current explanations for Earth's $\sim$5,000 mineral species invoke element availability, geological processes, and evolutionary time. However, these factors do not explain why the number is finite and specifically bounded around this magnitude. We present an information-first perspective based on the Universal Binary Principle (UBP), demonstrating that mineral diversity is fundamentally constrained by \textit{geometric information capacity} in a 6D computational substrate, not by chemical or temporal factors. Our model predicts 3,000--10,000 stable minerals as a universal maximum, with Earth's observed diversity ($\sim$5,000) representing approximately 0.3\% of geometrically feasible crystal structures (1.5$\times$10$^6$), filtered by coherence requirements (NRCI $\geq$ 0.99), Triad Graph Interaction Constraints (TGIC), and observer cost ($O \approx 3.78$). Remarkably, the complexity distribution power law exponent ($\alpha \approx 3.78$) exactly matches the observer cost, and the geometric bound exponent (0.27) approximates the Y constant (0.2647), suggesting these limits emerge from pure geometric necessity rather than material constraints.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The Mineral Diversity Puzzle}

Earth hosts approximately 5,000 recognized mineral species\cite{hazen2008,hazen2022}, with predictions of $\sim$6,500 total including undiscovered varieties\cite{hazen2015}. This number is neither trivially small (dozens) nor astronomically large (millions), yet no current theory adequately explains \textit{why} this specific magnitude emerges. Traditional explanations invoke:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Element availability}: Earth's crust contains $\sim$90 naturally occurring elements in varying abundances\cite{goldschmidt1954}.
\item \textbf{Geological processes}: Mineral evolution proceeds through 10 distinct stages over 4.5 Ga\cite{hazen2008}.
\item \textbf{Biological amplification}: $\sim$50\% of mineral diversity arose directly or indirectly from life\cite{hazen2022}.
\item \textbf{Thermodynamic constraints}: Pauling's rules and crystal structure stability limits\cite{pauling1929}.
\end{enumerate}

However, these explanations are fundamentally \textit{chemical and historical}, not \textit{informational}. They describe \textit{how} minerals form, not \textit{why} the total count is bounded.
